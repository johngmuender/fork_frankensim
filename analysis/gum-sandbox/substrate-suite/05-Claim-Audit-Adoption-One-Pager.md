## THE CLAIM AUDIT — a one-page capstone for any science, civics, or media-literacy course

**What this is.** A summative project in which the student takes ONE real claim — from news, school, social media, advertising, or a textbook — and subjects it to a professional-grade audit. It teaches the single most transferable intellectual skill we know how to assess: *separating what a claim says from what its evidence licenses.* No specialized science content is required; the method is the content.

**THE ASSIGNMENT (student-facing).** Choose one claim that matters to you. Produce **one page** containing, in order:

1. **The claim, quoted exactly** — verbatim, dated, source attributed.
2. **Its tag, defended in two sentences.** Tags: ⬛ *established* (independently replicated) · 🟩 *valid-given-assumptions* (airtight logic from stated premises) · 🟨 *estimate-with-stated-uncertainty* · 🟦 *computed by a locked recipe* · 🟪 *borrowed from an authority whose premises you must check* · 🟥 *a guess wearing confidence*.
3. **One audit step, executed, work shown.** Pick one: recompute a quoted number · check a cited authority's premises against this case · check the method's validity in this regime · verify the claim two independent ways. *"The claim survived my check" is a passing outcome.*
4. **The kill condition.** What specific observation would prove this claim wrong? If honestly none exists: say so, and state what that means.
5. **The date audit.** Was the claim made *before* or *after* the data it explains? Evidence required.
6. **The verdict** — five sentences: best support · biggest weakness · riskiest open question · **the strongest sentence the evidence licenses** (your "language lock") · **the tempting stronger sentence you decline to write.**

**GRADING (weights):** claim 10 · tag 20 · executed step 25 · kill condition 15 · date audit 10 · verdict 20. **Pass/fail overlay, absolute:** *would this page survive a reader who wants you to be wrong?* Any untagged assertion, any excuse-making, any gap-free "basically true" fails the overlay regardless of points.

**Instructor notes (reverse).** Announce the overlay in week one. Grade reasoning-plus-tags over answers (correct number, missing tag = half credit). Keep three anonymized anchor papers: an A (a real recomputation that changed the verdict), a B (right machinery, verdict slightly over-licensed — the common ceiling), and an F worth teaching (beautiful prose, zero executed checks). Presentations: 3 minutes, one question from a randomly assigned "adversarial reader." Alignment: NGSS practices 4, 7, 8; any information-literacy standard. **Origin note:** adapted from the capstone of a physics elective built around a speculative research program that pre-registers its own failure conditions; the rubric works because it grades the *shape of honesty*, which is subject-independent.
