# INSERT PACK K — "THE DAY THE THEORY DIED"
## For use if and only if: robust Σm_ν < 0.058 eV, normal ordering standing, no independent evolving-DE preference at the pre-bound threshold (SWP Branch K conditions verified — checklist inside)

### K.0 Before class: the instructor's five minutes

Read this section alone, before students arrive. Your students have spent a semester inside this theory's derivations. Some of them — the ones who did the belt trick five times, who felt the ¼ derivation click — will experience the news as a small grief, and a few will experience it as betrayal ("why did we learn a dead theory?"). **Your job today is not to console and not to celebrate. It is to demonstrate that this day was designed for** — that the program's entire architecture existed so that this exact morning would be *meaningful instead of embarrassing*. You have one advantage no other science teacher has ever had: the obituary was written before the death, and you are holding it. Teach from that fact. Do not improvise eulogies; do not soften the kill; do not let anyone — including yourself — reach for the escape hatch that the collaboration pre-disarmed. The lesson's thesis, which you should write on the board before anyone enters:

> **"Today we find out whether we meant it."**

**Verification checklist (complete before teaching; 10 min):** □ The result is from ≥2 independent pipelines at the pre-frozen robustness threshold (≥3σ, prior-stable) — check the collaboration's Branch-K confirmation memo, not a headline. □ Normal ordering stands (if inverted ordering was established, this is Branch I, different pack — the stake floor moves to ~0.10 eV). □ No independent w(z) ≠ −1 preference at ≥3σ with the growth-correlated sign (if there is, Branch A's frozen rule governs and the kill is *suspended, not voided* — teach the suspension, not this pack). □ The official Obituary O-1 has published (within its 90-day clock). If any box is unchecked: teach the Branch-W holding pattern instead (15-minute version at the end of this pack).

### K.1 The lesson plan (50 min)

**Opening (5 min) — the news, undecorated.** Project the actual survey result. Then project, side by side, the S1 wall chart your class built in week 13 — the stake box reading "Σm_ν ∈ [0.058, 0.11] eV, NO; kill: robust Σ < 0.058." Say only: *"The adjudicator has spoken. Read both. What happened?"* Let a student say the words "the stake was killed." It matters that a student says it first.

**Act I (10 min) — the pre-commitment performed.** Distribute Handout K-1 (below): the relevant excerpts of Obituary O-1, published this week by the collaboration — and beside them, the *hash receipt* showing the obituary's fingerprint was filed publicly years before the data. Students verify the structure matches what they were taught in week 13: retraction named precisely; survivors itemized; no rescue. Then the first discussion question, verbatim: *"You drafted this obituary yourself, in week 13, as an exercise. Compare your draft to the real one. What did the professionals include that you didn't — and what does that tell you about writing under impartiality?"* (Common finding: student drafts under-itemized the survivors; grief-writing forgets to be generous. The real O-1's survivor list is *long*, and its length is the day's first surprise.)

**Act II (15 min) — the autopsy, with tags.** On the board, rebuild the dependency chain as a class (this is the S-21 condition-stack, student edition): what exactly died, and what exactly didn't, and *why the boundary sits where it sits*. Walk it claim by claim, students calling tags:

*DEAD (retracted):* the heliknoton-as-neutrino construction (Ch. 14) — the mass came out wrong; the frustration→neutrino bridge (the logarithm) — its output is excluded; Stake S1 and prediction P-ν5; **and Chapter 13's family result reverts** — the board should show the exact demotion, verbatim from O-1: *"the lepton-spacing result reverts to an unexplained ≈2σ structural curiosity."* Not "wrong" — *unexplained*: the two integrals still landed the two mass gaps at 0.05σ and 0.4σ; what died is the claim that the mechanism *behind* them is understood, because that mechanism's one forward prediction just failed. (This distinction — the numbers stand, the explanation fell — is the autopsy's subtlest cut; give it three full minutes.)

*ALIVE (untouched, with reasons):* the photon theorems (Ch. 5 — never leaned on the mass sector); the closure, the ¼, √2, κ²g = 7/8, and ħ = 2.515√(Λ·J) (Ch. 10 — the bench stakes S3/S4′/S5 are *still live and still adjudicating*); gravity's slaving theorem and the Gibbs–Duhem defusal (Ch. 12); confinement's dichotomy (Ch. 15); the padlocks and the A3 gate (Ch. 16 — the arrival-time program proceeds regardless). The class should discover, by counting, that **most of the course survived** — and then be asked the harder question: *"Does the survival of Chapters 5–12 mean GUM is 'mostly fine'? Tag your answer."* (No — the surviving sectors are 🟩-within-a-model whose flagship falsifiable just failed; their *epistemic* position weakened even though their *logical* position is untouched. A theory that loses its only dated stake has lost its best claim on our attention, not its internal validity. Students who articulate this distinction have passed the course a second time.)

**Act III (10 min) — the disarmed hatch, honored.** Pose the temptation exactly as a motivated defender would: *"But wait — Chapter 12 says GUM's own dark energy relaxes the neutrino bound! Maybe Σm_ν isn't really below 0.058 in the true cosmology!"* Then make the class execute the pre-bound rule from problem 12.5's lesson: the escape is invocable *only if* the data independently prefer w(z) ≠ −1 at ≥3σ with the growth-correlated sign — and (per today's checklist) they don't. Board the frozen sentence: **"A kill under standard assumptions with no independent dark-energy signal IS a kill."** Then the debrief question that lands the whole semester: *"The collaboration wrote that sentence years ago, when it didn't know which way the data would cut. If they wrote it TODAY, could you trust it? Could THEY?"* (No, and no — and that asymmetry, decided-while-impartial versus decided-under-fire, is the single most transferable idea the course owns. Students met it in sports-excuse form in 12.5; today it performed at full scale, on the record, against its authors' interests.)

**Act IV (7 min) — what the field keeps.** Read aloud O-1's closing inventory (Handout K-1, section 3): the frozen pipeline's full records published "so that whatever replaces it starts from our wall, not our rubble"; the fourteen-kill registry now fifteen; the bench stakes still armed. Then the last discussion prompt: *"Name one thing physics is better off knowing today than it was yesterday — because of this failure."* Acceptable answers include: that the lepton-spacing coincidence is real and now *needs a new explanation* (a sharpened mystery is a gift); that helical-frustration mechanisms of this class are excluded in this regime (map of the wall); that pre-registration at program scale is *executable* (the method survived its own theory — arguably the biggest result of the day).

**Closing (3 min) — the margin note, performed.** In front of the class, take the classroom copy of the Primer, open Chapter 14, and physically affix the black-bordered margin insert (text below). Do not rewrite anything; do not remove the S1 wall chart — annotate it: a dated strip across the stake box reading **KILLED — [date] — see O-1.** Say the course's governing sentence one last time: *"Annotate, never rewrite. The book was honest when it was written; the honest thing now is the date, not the eraser."* Dismiss on time. Grief, if any, does its work better after a lesson that respected it than during one that milked it.

# INSERT PACK S — "THE DAY THE STAKE SURVIVED"
## For use if and only if: a robust cosmological determination lands Σm_ν inside [0.058, 0.11] eV with normal ordering (SWP Branch S conditions — checklist inside)

### S.0 Before class: the instructor's five minutes

Read alone, before students arrive. **Today's danger is the opposite of Pack K's, and it is larger.** Nobody needs help staying humble at a funeral; everybody needs help staying humble at a party. Your students — and you — will want today to mean "GUM is true." It does not, and the entire integrity of the course rides on the next 50 minutes proving that you meant THAT, too. The pre-commitments you must enforce against the room's joy: the ceiling sentence is **"the stake survived its adjudicator"** — never "confirmed," never "vindicated," never "proven"; the grade updates only by the frozen rule (survival + detection significance; internal ceiling ~3σ-equivalent — still below discovery); and *nothing stronger may be said by anyone* until the frozen pipeline has been **independently replicated by outsiders** — a mandate, not a courtesy. Board thesis, written before anyone enters:

> **"Today we find out whether we meant it — and surviving a test is the harder day to mean it on."**

**Verification checklist:** □ ≥2 independent pipelines, ≥3σ-class robustness, prior-stable, per the frozen definition. □ Central value inside [0.058, 0.11]; normal ordering preferred or established. □ The collaboration's Branch-S memo has issued with the ceiling language. □ Note the value's position in-band (center? edge? — you'll use it in Act II). If a box fails → Branch-W holding lesson (Pack K §K.4, mirrored).

### S.1 The lesson plan (50 min)

**Opening (5 min) — the news, undecorated.** Project the result beside the week-13 wall chart. Ask: *"What happened?"* A student will say "GUM was right." **Do not correct them yet.** Write the sentence on the board, attribute it ("Claim, offered by [name], tag pending"), and promise the class will grade it by the end of the period. (This suspended sentence is the lesson's spine.)

**Act I (10 min) — what a survived stake is worth, exactly.** Re-run the coin-sigma calibration live (Ch. 17's machinery, now load-bearing): the stake's window [0.058, 0.11] spans a modest slice of the plausible range; the prediction was self-graded ≈2σ standing alone; survival at the adjudicator upgrades by the frozen Bayes bookkeeping to — read it from the S-memo — **~3σ-equivalent internal ceiling.** Then the calibration question: *"Three sigma is a one-in-370 fluke rate. The Higgs required five. In our week-15 coin experiment, how many classrooms of coin-flippers does 3σ correspond to?"* (One 8-heads-plus student in ~15 classrooms — rare, real-feeling, and *still not discovery-grade*.) Board the ladder: 2σ = interesting → 3σ = evidence → 5σ = discovery — **"today we climbed one rung, not the ladder."**

**Act II (15 min) — the adversarial celebration.** Split the room. **Team Advocate** gets 5 minutes to make the honest maximal case: the number was computed from *charged lepton masses alone* through one logarithm; the pipeline was frozen and hashed before the data; the kill condition was real and was risked; the center held under a sixfold error-shrink; and now nature's measurement landed in the staked band — this is what a mechanism *being right* would look like. **Team Auditor** gets 5 minutes to make the honest deflationary case, and must land all four of: (i) the 0.05 eV scale was world-public before the pipeline froze — only the *pipeline* was blind, not the target; (ii) a ±0.90 logarithm is a wide net — the corpus itself graded the landing's prior weight at ~16%-class, "1σ-trivia" on proximity alone; (iii) confirmation-by-band is weaker than confirmation-by-point — where in the band did the value land, and would *any* in-band value have been claimed as success? (yes — which is honest, and limits the update); (iv) the mechanism has post-hoc scars on record (E-F1) and a coin-flip flag (F12) — survival doesn't launder those grades. You adjudicate with the frozen verdict, verbatim from the S-memo: **"the stake survived its adjudicator; external replication is now mandatory before any stronger sentence."** Then grade the opening student's suspended claim ("GUM was right") as a class: it fails the language lock; the licensed sentence goes up beside it; both stay on the board, labeled — the tempting sentence and the licensed one, the final project's rubric performed at civilization scale.

**Act III (10 min) — what happens next, by the book.** Walk the pre-committed Branch-S sequence: (1) the collaboration's replication *invitation* (pre-drafted years ago) issues to outside groups — the frozen pipeline's full records, escrow hashes, and covariance data are already public for exactly this moment; (2) the S1 wall chart gets its dated green strip: **SURVIVED — [date] — replication pending**; (3) the *other* stakes' status is read aloud — S2's HL-LHC nulls, the bench's S3/S4′/S5, S6's slow burn, the A3 gate — because a survived S1 *raises* the stakes on every remaining line rather than retiring them: *"a theory that just earned attention now owes MORE tests, not fewer."* Discussion prompt: *"Which surviving stake would now be most damning if it failed — and did today's news change your week-15 answer?"* (Defensible shift: the bench stakes gain teeth — a theory with a surviving cosmological stake that then flunks its own exact ¼ on a tabletop would be in a uniquely instructive kind of trouble.)

**Act IV (7 min) — the hard question, faced on the good day.** Pose it plainly: *"Suppose replication succeeds, S2's nulls hold, the bench delivers ¼ and 7/8, and the A3 gate opens. At what point would you say the vacuum IS a chiral solid — and who decides?"* Let the discussion run genuinely open; close with the course's honest position: the tags have a ceiling too — ⬛ is earned by *independent replication across methods and communities over time*, not by any single day, and the sociology of that earning (review, replication, rival explanations exhausted) is itself part of how science knows. Today moved one tag one notch. That is what a very good day looks like from inside honest science — and the fact that it feels slightly anticlimactic is the feeling of the method *working*.

**Closing (3 min) — the margin note, performed.** Affix the green-bordered insert to Chapter 14 in front of the class (text below); annotate — never rewrite — the wall chart. Final sentence, scripted: *"In week one I promised you I'd answer 'is it true?' the same way in week sixteen. Here it is: nobody knows — but as of today, the theory has survived the one test it scheduled with strangers, and it has more appointments. THAT sentence, and not a louder one, is what we earned. Class dismissed."*
