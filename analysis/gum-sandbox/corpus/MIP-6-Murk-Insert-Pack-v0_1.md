## TEACHING NOTES (dated margin notes; verbatim-affixable)

> **Primer, the cosmology chapter · [beside the dark-matter passage] — "The fog's frozen knots."** When the universe's last symmetry broke — parity, at the ten-MeV epoch — the condensing mist knotted, the way every hurried freeze knots. Most of what it made unraveled or sang itself away in its own dark radiation. What could survive was the quietest object the theory owns: a neutral knot, two hard cores on a soft tether, invisible to light *for the same reason the mist itself is*. The theory can prove the knots exist; whether they are the quarter of the sky we call dark matter hangs on two computations it names, prices, and has not done — and it says exactly that, at full size, which is rarer than any discovery.
>
> **Primer, same chapter · [a second margin note] — "The theory that promised you nothing in your detector."** GUM's dark matter cannot ring an underground detector — not this decade, not ever; its cross-section hides below the neutrino fog itself. Forty years of silence is what its arithmetic *looks like*. Notice the asymmetry, because it is the whole epistemology in one line: **the silence can never confirm the murk, but any bell that rings for a rival kills it.** A candidate that can only lose the channels everyone watches, and can only win in two narrow lights nobody has derived yet — that is not a weakness of the filing; that is the filing.
>
> **Primer margin · [beside the lifetime passage] — "One number, three fates."** A single geometric quantity — the energy of the knot's core, in natural units — decides whether the murk died young, glows darkly on a hundred-billion-year fuse, or outlives every star. The computation is assigned. Until it lands, the theory holds all three obituaries drafted, which is the only honest way to hold a razor.
>
