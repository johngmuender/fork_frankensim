## TEACHING NOTES (dated margin notes; verbatim-affixable)

> **Primer, the cosmology chapter · [beside the dark-matter passage] — "The hole with a name on it."** A quarter of the universe is missing, and this theory cannot supply it — its own theorems close the cupboard, twice. Most frameworks in that position quietly bend something. This one filed the hole under a flag (F19), gave it a specification card (what the missing thing must weigh, how cold it must be, what it must not touch), stapled a bill to it (the strange Hubble-scale acceleration written on every disk galaxy — someone owes that number a story), and shelved it beside its two siblings: *what we cannot compute, what we will not assume, what we do not yet have.* A hole with a name, a shape, and an invoice is not a failure of a theory; it is a theory keeping books.
>
> **Primer, same chapter · [a second margin note] — "The theory that would rather be incomplete than wrong."** There is a famous alternative: change gravity so the missing mass was never missing. This theory *cannot take that road* — not from timidity but from its own derivation: the 1/r law is welded to the same chain that produced everything else, and a linear-potential escape would require forbidding the very term the substrate insists on producing. So the theory chose the harder honesty: **incompleteness over contortion.** Watch how rarely that choice gets made in public.
>
> **Primer margin · [beside the trinity shelf] — "Three kinds of honest boundary."** F17: a regime the mathematics abandons. F18: an assumption the theory declines to sign. F19: a thing the data demand and the theory has not built. Learn to tell them apart in any theory you ever read — most frameworks have all three and label none.
>
> **Course, closing-chapter exercise proposals (★★):** (i) Prove the lock's structural clause: why a γr potential requires the Einstein term *forbidden*, not merely small — and why that makes the lock a theorem rather than an inventory. (ii) Derive, in three sentences, why the CMB's demand for non-baryonic clustering matter is independent of every rotation curve ever measured. (iii) **The CA exercise:** write a full divergence row for two frameworks you believe *unequally* — without scoring either; the discipline is harder than it sounds and is the whole point. (iv) **The invoice exercise:** explain why converting a seductive regularity into a demand on your own missing sector (q-r → d-G1) is stronger discipline than either deriving it or ignoring it — and identify the failure mode each easier road hides.
